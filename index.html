Unit 5 · Sub-Unit Study

A Baker from Goa

In Chapter 5 of First Flight, author Lucio Rodrigues nostalgically recounts the childhood companion of every Goan village: the pader (village baker). He arrived with the musical jhang-jhang of his specially carved bamboo staff, delivering warm crusty loaves, bangles of bread for children, and rich festive sweet bread called bol.

The Pader's Attire
The traditional kabai (a peculiar one-piece frock) and knee-length trousers that made bakers unmistakable figures.
Indispensable Feasts
Marriage engagements were incomplete without bolinhas, and Christmas required cakes and fresh loaves baked in clay ovens.

Literary Theme: Living cultural micro-history preserved through colonial memory and communal friendship.

Goan Folk Heritage

Folk Dances of Goa

Goan folk traditions capture the lively interplay of native Konkani agrarian rhythms and 450 years of Portuguese courtly styling.

Fugdi & Dhalo
Performed exclusively by women during Ganeshotsav and harvest. Dancers form rotating circles, clapping rhythmically and blowing breathy sounds into their hands in an accelerating tempo.
Dekhnni & Corridinho
Dekhnni embodies temple dancers crossing the river to a Hindu wedding, accompanied by the clay Ghumot. Corridinho showcases Portuguese ballroom partner footwork in swirling skirts.

Major Exponents: Goa Cultural Academy and traditional Ghumot masters preserving village rhythms.

Mandatory Paired State

Introduction to Sikkim

Nestled beneath the majestic massif of Mount Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), Sikkim is a sacred Himalayan realm where nature and Vajrayana Buddhism converge. Its people comprise the indigenous Lepcha, the Bhutia, and the vibrant Nepali communities.

Sacred Cham Dances
Monks in elaborate silk brocade and carved wooden masks perform slow, meditative turns to invoke blessings and banish malevolence.
Singhi Chham & Maruni
The mythical Snow Lion dance honors Kanchenjunga as protector deity, while Maruni brings celebratory Nepali harvest cheer.

Monastic Sanctuaries: Rumtek, Pemayangtse, Enchey, and Tashiding Gompas anchor the spiritual landscape.

Comparative Chronology

Historical Evolution

Contrasting the developmental timeline of performative folk rituals across maritime Goa and high-altitude Sikkim.

16th – 18th Century Goa (Colonial Confluence): Portuguese arrival in 1510 merged European chordophones (mandolins, violins) with the native terracotta Ghumot pot drum, generating Mando ballroom serenades alongside agrarian Fugdi twirls.
8th – 17th Century Sikkim (Tantric Consecration): Guru Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche) introduced sacred Cham masked dances in the 8th century to consecrate the land; formal monastic choreographies were later codified under Chogyal Phuntsog Namgyal in 1642.

Mandatory Art Form · 3 Marks

Culinary Lore & Bread

Curated by Ananya Deshmukh (Roll 10102). A deep dive into the comparative food science between the wood-fired ovens of coastal Goa and the steaming vessels of alpine Sikkim.

Goan Yeast Heritage
Portuguese monks taught Goan bakers to ferment flour using local coconut toddy, yielding crusty Pao, hollow pocket Poi, and sweet Bolinhas.
Sikkimese Fermentation
To survive sub-zero winters, Sikkim developed gut-healthy lactic fermentation: Gundruk (sun-dried greens), steaming Momos, and warming Thukpa noodle broth.

Artistic Contribution: Demonstrating live breadmaking physics and Himalayan vegetable fermentation methods.

Individual Art Form Showcase

Thangka & Azulejos

Presented by Rohan Kulkarni (Roll 10103). Comparing Goa's glazed blue ceramic Azulejos tile art with Sikkim's sacred scroll paintings (Thangkas) and sacred Mandala geometry.

Sikkimese Thangka Sacred Geometry
Drawn on stretched cotton scrolls according to strict iconometric ratios laid down in Buddhist scripture. Pigments are ground from real lapis lazuli, cinnabar, malachite, and 24K gold dust.
Goan Azulejos Ceramic Tilework
Hand-painted cobalt blue and white glazed tin tiles decorating church portals and historic villas, depicting coastal fishermen, caravels, and village baker journeys.

Medium: Natural gouache, mineral pigments, and mathematical grid studies.

Individual Art Form Showcase

Heritage Architecture

Researched by Kabir Sharma (Roll 10105). A travel monograph contrasting Old Goa's Baroque stone cathedrals with Sikkim's earthquake-resilient timber Gompas.

Basilica of Bom Jesus (Goa)
Completed in 1605 from exposed reddish-brown laterite stone, featuring Corinthian pilasters and Baroque gilded altars holding St. Francis Xavier's relics.
Rumtek & Pemayangtse (Sikkim)
Multilevel timber structures with interlocking joinery to withstand Himalayan tremors, crowned by golden pagoda finials and wide courtyard dance arenas.

Structural Synthesis: Both styles prioritize communal courtyards for sacred festivals and music.

Mandatory Comparative Study · Page 1 & 3

Comparative Matrix

Synthesizing cultural, performative, gastronomic, and geographic distinctions between Goa and Sikkim.

Goa (Fugdi & Dekhnni):

Fast-paced social circle dance with hand clapping, accompanied by clay Ghumot pots and Western violin harmonies.

Sikkim (Cham & Maruni):

Meditative Buddhist mask dance honoring Padmasambhava, accompanied by long copper dungchen horns and 32-peg Damphu drums.

Goa:

Stone-baked Pao, Poi, Bolinhas, coconut fish curry, and spiced toddy-fermented delicacies.

Sikkim:

Steamed Momos, warming Thukpa broth, fermented Gundruk greens, and festive sweet Sel Roti.

Goa:

Red-checkered cotton Kunbi saree knotted at shoulder; baker's historical single-piece kabai frock.

Sikkim:

Silk Bakhu wrap-around robe with Honju blouse, striped woven Pangden aprons, and turquoise Dzi beads.

Goa:

Arabian Sea coastline, Mandovi river estuaries, tropical palm groves, warm humid maritime weather.

Sikkim:

Eastern Himalayas, Teesta river canyon, alpine meadows, Mount Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), sub-zero snowfields.
